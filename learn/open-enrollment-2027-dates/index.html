Open Enrollment 2027: key dates and what to gather

The dates that matter for 2027 health coverage, in plain English, plus a calm list of what to have on hand before you look at plans.

Last updated: November 1, 2026 · By Suzelaine Similien, Fern & Fine Print

  1. Home
  2. Learn
  3. Open Enrollment 2027: key dates and what to gather

The short answer

For states that use HealthCare.gov, Open Enrollment for 2027 Marketplace health plans runs from November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027. Pick a plan by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1; pick one between December 16 and January 15 and coverage starts February 1. Some states run their own marketplace and can set different deadlines, and Medicare and job-based plans have their own separate windows. (HealthCare.gov dates and deadlines)

When is Open Enrollment for 2027 health coverage?

Here are the HealthCare.gov dates for 2027 coverage, as listed on HealthCare.gov:

HealthCare.gov Open Enrollment dates for 2027 coverage
DateWhat it means
November 1, 2026Open Enrollment starts. First day to enroll in, renew, or change a Marketplace plan for 2027.
December 15, 2026Last day to enroll or change plans for coverage that starts January 1, 2027.
January 1, 2027Coverage starts for people who enrolled by December 15 and paid their first premium.
January 15, 2027Open Enrollment ends. After this, you can generally enroll or change plans only with a Special Enrollment Period.
February 1, 2027Coverage starts for people who enrolled December 16 through January 15 and paid their first premium.

One small detail people miss: coverage starts only after you pay your first premium to the insurance company, not just after you pick a plan.

Does my state use HealthCare.gov or its own marketplace?

It depends on where you live. Some states run their own marketplace website instead of HealthCare.gov, and a state-run marketplace can set its own Open Enrollment dates. HealthCare.gov has a state-by-state page that tells you where people in your state apply. For example, HealthCare.gov notes that starting November 1, Oregon residents use ExploreHealthOR.gov to enroll in 2027 coverage.

Tip: if your state runs its own marketplace, look up the deadline on that official state site and write it down. Don't assume it matches the HealthCare.gov dates.

I already have a Marketplace plan. Do I need to do anything?

It's worth looking. According to HealthCare.gov, by November 1 you should get two letters, one from your insurance company and one from the Marketplace. Together they tell you whether your current plan is available next year, which plan (if any) you'd be automatically re-enrolled in if you don't act by December 15, and any changes to your coverage and savings.

Plans, networks, drug lists, and prices can change every year, and so can your own situation. HealthCare.gov also notes that you should update your application so your savings are figured correctly, and that in some cases you won't be automatically re-enrolled.

What should I gather before I look at plans?

HealthCare.gov publishes an application checklist (PDF). In everyday terms, it helps to have:

  • Basic details for everyone in your household, including people not applying, plus home and mailing addresses.
  • Social Security numbers (and document information for anyone with eligible immigration status who is applying).
  • How you file taxes, and who you claim as dependents.
  • Income information, like recent pay stubs or tax forms, and your best estimate of your household income for 2027.
  • Any current coverage, including policy numbers, and information about any job-based coverage you could get (even if you don't take it).
  • Any notices about a health reimbursement arrangement (HRA) from an employer.

And for comparing plans (not on the official checklist, but it makes shopping much calmer):

  • A list of the doctors, specialists, hospitals, and pharmacy you want to keep.
  • Your prescriptions, with the exact name and dose.
  • Any care you expect next year, like a planned surgery, a pregnancy, therapy, or regular specialist visits.

How is Medicare Open Enrollment different?

Medicare has its own calendar. According to Medicare.gov, the Medicare Open Enrollment Period runs October 15 to December 7 each year. During it, people with Medicare can join, switch, or drop a Medicare Advantage Plan or Medicare drug plan, with coverage starting January 1. There's also a separate Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period from January 1 to March 31 for people already in a Medicare Advantage Plan. Free, unbiased Medicare counseling is available through your state's State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP).

What about open enrollment at work?

Job-based plans set their own open enrollment window, and it may not match the Marketplace dates. HealthCare.gov's glossary says to check with your employer. Your HR or benefits office can tell you your exact dates.

What happens if I miss Open Enrollment?

After January 15, you can generally enroll in or change a Marketplace plan only if you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, usually because of a life event like losing other coverage, moving, getting married, or having or adopting a baby. Depending on the type, you usually have 60 days before or after the event. You can apply for Medicaid or CHIP any time of year.

Quick questions

When is Open Enrollment for 2027?

For HealthCare.gov states, Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs from November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027. State-run marketplaces can set different dates.

What is the deadline for coverage starting January 1, 2027?

On HealthCare.gov, December 15, 2026 is the last day to enroll in or change plans for coverage that starts January 1, 2027, as long as you pay your first premium.

Can I still enroll after January 15?

Generally only if you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period because of a life event, like losing other coverage, moving, getting married, or having a baby. Medicaid and CHIP accept applications any time.

Is Medicare Open Enrollment the same as Marketplace Open Enrollment?

No. Medicare's Open Enrollment Period runs October 15 to December 7 each year and is separate from the Marketplace window.

Will I be automatically re-enrolled in my Marketplace plan?

Many people are, if they don't act by December 15, but not in every case. Your Open Enrollment letters say which plan, if any, you'd be re-enrolled in. Updating your application helps make sure your savings are figured correctly.
