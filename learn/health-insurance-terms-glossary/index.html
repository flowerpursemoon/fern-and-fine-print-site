Health insurance terms glossary

The words on your plan documents, translated. No jargon required to read the jargon.

Last updated: November 1, 2026 · By Suzelaine Similien, Fern & Fine Print

The short answer

The five words that explain most of what a health plan costs: your premium (monthly bill), deductible (what you pay before the plan shares costs), copay (a flat amount per service), coinsurance (a percentage you pay after the deductible), and out-of-pocket maximum (the yearly ceiling on covered, in-network costs, not counting premiums). Everything else on this page builds on those. (HealthCare.gov glossary)

Health insurance terms, A to Z

Plain-English definitions, based on the official HealthCare.gov glossary unless noted. Use your browser's find feature to jump to a word.

Allowed amount
The most a plan will pay for a covered service. It may also be called the eligible expense, payment allowance, or negotiated rate. If a provider charges more, you may have to pay the difference. (official definition)
Balance billing
When a provider bills you for the difference between their charge and the plan's allowed amount. A preferred (in-network) provider may not balance bill you for covered services. (official definition)
Catastrophic plan
A Marketplace plan with a lower premium and higher out-of-pocket costs that covers 3 primary care visits a year before the deductible (plus preventive care). It's available to people under 30, or older people with a hardship or affordability exemption. (official definition)
COBRA
A federal option that may let you keep your job-based health plan for a while after you leave a job. You usually pay the full premium yourself, including the part your employer used to pay. (official definition)
Coinsurance
Your share of the cost of a covered service as a percentage, like 20%, usually after you've paid your deductible. (official definition)
Copayment (copay)
A fixed amount, like $20, that you pay for a covered service. Copays can be different for different services in the same plan. (official definition)
Cost-sharing reductions (extra savings)
A discount that lowers your deductibles, copays, and coinsurance if you qualify based on income. In the Marketplace you get them only if you enroll in a Silver plan. (official definition)
Deductible
What you pay for covered services before your plan starts to pay its share. Certain preventive services are covered before the deductible. (official definition)
Exclusive Provider Organization (EPO)
A plan that covers services only from doctors, specialists, and hospitals in its network, except in an emergency. (official definition)
Excluded services
Health care services your plan doesn't pay for or cover. (official definition)
Explanation of Benefits (EOB)
A statement your health plan sends after you get care, showing what the provider billed, what the plan paid, and what you may owe. It isn't a bill; compare it with any bill you receive.
Formulary
The list of prescription drugs a plan covers. Also called a drug list. (official definition)
Health Maintenance Organization (HMO)
A plan that usually limits coverage to doctors who work for or contract with the HMO and generally won't cover out-of-network care except in an emergency. (official definition)
Health Savings Account (HSA)
A savings account for qualified medical expenses using pre-tax money. You can contribute only if you have an HSA-eligible (high deductible) plan. HSA funds generally can't be used for premiums. (official definition)
High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)
A plan with a higher deductible and usually a lower premium that can be paired with an HSA. Often called an HSA-eligible plan. (official definition)
In-network / out-of-network
In-network providers have a contract with your plan, which usually means lower costs for you. Out-of-network care usually costs more, may not be covered at all, and generally doesn't count toward your out-of-pocket maximum. (official definition)
Medicaid
A joint federal and state program that provides free or low-cost coverage to some people with low incomes, families and children, pregnant women, the elderly, and people with disabilities. You can apply any time. (official definition)
Medicare
Federal health insurance for people 65 and older, certain younger people with disabilities, and people with End-Stage Renal Disease. It isn't part of the Marketplace. (official definition)
Metal levels (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum)
Marketplace plan categories that show how you and the plan share costs. They have nothing to do with quality of care. (official definition)
Network
The doctors, hospitals, and other providers your plan has contracted with. (official definition)
Open Enrollment Period
The yearly window to enroll in or change a Marketplace plan. For HealthCare.gov states it runs November 1 to January 15. Job-based plans and Medicare have their own windows. (official definition)
Out-of-pocket maximum
The most you pay for covered, in-network services in a plan year. After you reach it, the plan pays 100% of covered costs. Premiums don't count toward it. (official definition)
Plan year
The 12-month period your benefits cover. For job-based plans it may not match the calendar year. (official definition)
Point of Service (POS)
A plan where you pay less using in-network providers and need a referral from your primary care doctor to see a specialist. (official definition)
Preferred Provider Organization (PPO)
A plan where you pay less using in-network providers but can use out-of-network providers without a referral for an additional cost. (official definition)
Premium
What you pay for your health insurance every month, whether or not you use care. (official definition)
Premium tax credit
A tax credit that can lower your monthly Marketplace premium, based on your household information and estimated income. If you use more in advance than you qualify for, you may owe the difference at tax time. (official definition)
Preventive services
Routine care like screenings, check-ups, and counseling to prevent illness. Most plans must cover a set of preventive services at no cost in network. (official definition)
Prior authorization
Approval a plan may require before you get a service or fill a prescription for it to be covered. (official definition)
Referral
A written order from your primary care doctor to see a specialist or get certain services. Many HMOs require one. (official definition)
Special Enrollment Period (SEP)
A time outside Open Enrollment when you can sign up because of a life event, like losing coverage, moving, marriage, or a new baby. You usually have 60 days. (official definition)
Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)
A standard, easy-to-read summary of a plan's costs and coverage that lets you compare plans apples to apples. (official definition)

Quick questions

What is a premium?

Your premium is the amount you pay for health insurance every month, whether or not you use care.

What is a copay?

A copay is a fixed amount, like $20, that you pay for a covered service. Copays can differ for different services in the same plan.

What is the difference between a copay and coinsurance?

A copay is a flat dollar amount per service. Coinsurance is a percentage of the allowed amount, like 20%, usually after you meet your deductible.

What is an Explanation of Benefits?

An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is a statement from your plan showing what was billed, what the plan paid, and what you may owe. It isn't a bill.

What is an SBC?

A Summary of Benefits and Coverage is a standard, easy-to-read summary of a plan's costs and coverage that helps you compare plans apples to apples.
