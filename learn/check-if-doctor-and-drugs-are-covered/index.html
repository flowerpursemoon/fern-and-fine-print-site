How to check if your doctor and drugs are covered

Before you pick a plan, make sure the people and prescriptions you rely on come with it.

Last updated: November 1, 2026 · By Suzelaine Similien, Fern & Fine Print

  1. Home
  2. Learn
  3. How to check if your doctor and drugs are covered

The short answer

Look up the exact plan name and year in that plan's provider directory and its drug list (called a formulary). Then confirm with your doctor's office and with the plan itself, because networks and drug lists can change. For each drug, also note its tier and whether it needs prior authorization. Medicare.gov gives the same core advice for Medicare plans: add your drugs when comparing plans, and ask your doctors and pharmacies if they're in network. (HealthCare.gov: formulary · Medicare.gov)

Why does the exact plan name matter?

A plan's network is the set of doctors, hospitals, and other providers it has contracted with. One insurance company can offer several plans with different networks, so "my doctor takes that insurance company" doesn't always mean "my doctor is in this plan's network." Always check the specific plan name, and the year you're enrolling for.

How do I check if my doctor is in network?

  1. Write down your list: primary doctor, specialists, hospital, urgent care, lab, and pharmacy. Include each provider's full name and office location.
  2. Search the plan's provider directory. Marketplace plan details link to each plan's directory. Search by the provider's name and the location you actually visit.
  3. Call your doctor's office. Ask: "Are you in network with [exact plan name] for [year]?" Ask about the specific location too.
  4. Confirm with the plan. Call the number in the plan details and ask the same question. Write down the date, the person's name, and any reference number.
Why both calls? Directories can be out of date, and an office may accept some plans from an insurer but not others. Two confirmations and a note of who you spoke with give you a paper trail.

How do I check if my medication is covered?

  1. List each prescription with its exact name, strength, and how often you take it.
  2. Find the plan's formulary (drug list), linked from the plan details, for the year you're enrolling in.
  3. Look up each drug and note its tier (tiers usually mean different costs) and any notes like prior authorization, step therapy, or quantity limits.
  4. Check the cost in the plan's Summary of Benefits and Coverage, and whether drugs have a separate deductible.
  5. Ask your pharmacy if it's in the plan's network.

Prior authorization means the plan may need to approve a service or prescription before it's covered. If a drug isn't on the list, ask your doctor whether a covered alternative would work for you, and ask the plan how it handles exceptions.

What questions should I ask the plan?

  • "Is [doctor or hospital] at [location] in network for [exact plan name] in [year]?"
  • "Do I need a referral to see a specialist?"
  • "Is [drug, strength] on the formulary? What tier? Does it need prior authorization, step therapy, or have quantity limits?"
  • "Is my pharmacy in network? Is there a mail-order option?"
  • "Is there a separate deductible for prescription drugs?"

What if my doctor isn't in the network?

It depends on the plan type. HMOs and EPOs generally don't cover out-of-network care except in an emergency. PPOs let you use out-of-network providers for an additional cost, and POS plans pay less when you stay in network. Out-of-network costs generally don't count toward your out-of-pocket maximum, and an out-of-network provider may bill you for the difference between their charge and the plan's allowed amount (balance billing).

There is a federal safety net for some situations. According to CMS, the No Surprises Act protects people with most types of health insurance from unexpected out-of-network bills for emergency room visits, certain non-emergency care related to a visit to an in-network hospital or surgery center, and air ambulance services.

Checking doctors and drugs for a Medicare plan

Medicare.gov suggests adding all the prescription drugs you take regularly when you compare plans, asking your doctors and pharmacies if they're in network with the plan, and checking whether the plan covers you if you live in another state part of the year. Free counseling is available through your state's SHIP. (Medicare.gov)

Quick questions

How do I know if my doctor is in network?

Search the provider directory for the exact plan name and year, then confirm by calling both the doctor's office and the plan. Note the date, who you spoke with, and any reference number.

How do I find out if my medication is covered?

Look up the drug in the plan's formulary (drug list) for the year you're enrolling in. Note its tier and whether it needs prior authorization, step therapy, or has quantity limits, then confirm with the plan.

What is a formulary?

A formulary is the list of prescription drugs a plan covers. It's also called a drug list.

What happens if I see an out-of-network doctor?

HMOs and EPOs generally don't cover out-of-network care except in emergencies. PPOs do, for an additional cost. Out-of-network costs generally don't count toward your out-of-pocket maximum.

Does the No Surprises Act protect me from surprise bills?

For most types of health insurance, it protects you from unexpected out-of-network bills for emergency room visits, certain non-emergency care related to a visit to an in-network hospital or surgery center, and air ambulance services.
